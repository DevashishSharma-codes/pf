Collaborative whiteboard tools like Excalidraw and Figma look deceptively simple on the surface. But under the hood, building a low-latency multi-user canvas requires managing geometry math, ray-casting hit detection, delta synchronization, and conflict resolution across distributed clients.

With [Picasso](https://cali-web-one.vercel.app/), I set out to architect a full-stack, real-time diagramming canvas inside a **Turborepo monorepo** with **sub-50ms broadcast latency**.

---

### Core Tech Stack

- **Frontend:** Next.js 16, React 19, TypeScript, Rough.js, HTML5 Canvas API
- **State & Auth:** Clerk Authentication, Zustand, WebSocket client
- **Backend & Database:** Node.js WebSockets server, PostgreSQL, Prisma ORM, Supabase storage
- **Monorepo:** Turborepo + pnpm workspace structure

---

### Geometry & Hit-Testing Engine

Instead of relying on heavy DOM elements for thousands of shapes, Picasso renders everything to an optimized HTML5 Canvas using vector projection:

- **Ray-Casting Polygon Detection:** Point-in-polygon algorithms to detect clicks on complex multi-point freehand paths and rotated polygons.
- **8-Point Bounding Box Resizing:** Accurate affine transformation matrices for uniform and non-uniform scaling with live rotation snapping.
- **Rough.js Hand-Drawn Aesthetics:** Custom stroke generation that retains the warm, human feel of rough sketches without sacrificing 60fps render loops.

---

### Real-Time Synchronization Architecture

To achieve sub-50ms latency:

1. **Optimistic Local Updates:** Cursor and shape movements render locally at display refresh rates (60–120Hz).
2. **Delta Broadcasting:** Only shape coordinates and action deltas (not full canvas state) are broadcast over WebSockets to active room peers.
3. **Prisma & Postgres Snapshotting:** Debounced state snapshots are persisted periodically to PostgreSQL, allowing users to leave and resume their canvas at any time.
