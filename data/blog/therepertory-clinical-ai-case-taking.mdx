Building consumer AI apps is vastly different from building AI tools for healthcare professionals. When practicing doctors rely on software during active patient consultations, **speed, deterministic reproducibility, and workflow ergonomics** matter far more than flashy demos.

During my Full Stack Internship at **21Spheres**, I owned the end-to-end delivery of the AI case-taking and Repertory diagnostic engine on [TheRepertory](https://stage.therepertory.com/).

---

### Understanding the Clinical Workflow

In classical repertorization, clinicians analyze patient symptom complexes across multiple philosophical repertory methodologies (including Kent, Boger, Boenninghausen, and others). 

Previously, searching across these disparate methodologies was fractured and time-consuming. We addressed this by:

1. **Expanding Methodological Coverage (1 → 8+):**
   Coordinating with UI/UX, product, and data teams to structure remedy datasets across 8+ standard clinical methodologies into unified schemas.

2. **Real-Time Q&A & Remedy-Ranking Pipeline:**
   Designing an interactive query and ranking pipeline that assists the clinician in formulating symptom rubrics in real-time, drastically cutting consultation prep time.

3. **Resilient WebSocket Sessions:**
   Patient queues and live case sessions depend on robust WebSockets. Fixing stale closure bugs and reconnection backoffs prevented clinician disconnections during active sessions.

4. **Production CI/CD to AWS ECS via ECR:**
   Prioritizing automated testing (60+ Jest test suites) and a continuous integration pipeline targeting AWS ECS containers, ensuring healthcare-grade stability on every release.

---

### Key Takeaway

Technical excellence in production healthcare software is about **empathy with clinical users**: streamlining complex domain methodologies into dependable, low-latency interfaces that doctors can trust.
